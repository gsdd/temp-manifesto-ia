## Why v3 exists

Gary's concern after reviewing v2: the nav may have taken Andy's services deck wording too literally. v2 had a written rule that "service labels are used exactly as in the Source B deck. Do not abbreviate or rename in the nav." That rule is what v3 removes.

The evidence is all in the 7 September call (Source C) and Gary's v3 brief (Source E):

- Andy and Gary agreed on search-friendly, plain language that normal people and Google understand.
- HPX failed as a term. Clients look for CX.
- Experience Engineering especially needs subtitles and keywords so that "can you build a website?" and CX and research map clearly.
- The services deck is a starting point for alignment. It is not website copy.
- Andy: "simplification of language is helpful".

The triangle itself does not change. Three columns, Growth Strategy first, Activation Services second, CEO Advisory third and quieter, expertise and sectors out of the mega-nav. v3 changes what the words in those columns say.

## The rule v3 applies

For every label in the header, mega-nav and footer, ask: **what would a prospect who has never heard of Manifesto type into Google to find this?**

| If | Then | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The deck label and the searched term are the same or close | Keep the deck label | Growth Strategy, CEO Advisory, AI Enablement |
| The deck label is a Manifesto term or product name that Andy wants to be recognised for, and no single plain term replaces it | Keep the deck label as the primary label and add a plain one-line subtitle that carries the searched terms | Experience Engineering, Growth Office, Side-by-Side, Proposition Innovation, Activation Services |
| The deck label and the searched term conflict, and the plain term is what the service actually is | Rename to the plain term. Keep the deck term on the service page (an "also known as" line) and as a redirect | Customer Intelligence, Data Agents, Operating Architecture |
| The label is an acronym or internal shorthand | Remove it from the nav | (SxS), HPX |

Every item in the mega-nav carries a subtitle regardless, because even plain labels benefit from a line that says who it is for and what it covers. Subtitles are fixed text, one line, no more than ten words, and they are where the search vocabulary lives (customer experience, website, research, loyalty, membership, subscription, AI, operating model, interim, retainer).
